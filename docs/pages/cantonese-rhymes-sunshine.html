What this is, and what it isn't

The seller's card is 30 tracks. One of them, 五隻鴨仔, is already on cantonese-nursery-rhymes, so it is dropped here rather than duplicated — that card keeps the 2:34 recording, this one skips the song entirely.

The seller's audio is trimmed, so several durations run longer here (十二隻恐龙 2:14 there, 4:13 on the official video). The songs are the same; the files are not. If a card must match their runtime exactly, the audio has to be cut down after download.

Two titles differ from the seller's list, and the reference is honest about it:

  • their 貼心歌 (1:53) matches the channel's 點心歌 (2:04) — dim sum song; there is no upload called 貼心歌.
  • their 何家公雞何家猜 is the channel's 何家小雞何家猜 (2:45).

How to use it

This is a collection, not a lesson. It is the card to put on when he asks for music, not the one to run a target from. The teaching cards stay speech-therapy-cantonese and speech-therapy-cantonese-routines.

Two habits make a difference with a collection this size:

  1. Play the same three songs for a fortnight. A 29-track card invites scrolling; repetition is what turns a song into words.
  2. Sing one line and stop. The gap is the invitation — 有隻八腳蜘蛛 and 老伯塊田係牧場 both have built-in pauses for exactly this.

The best tracks for him right now are the short, action-led ones: 去街市 (1:09), 有隻八腳蜘蛛 (1:41), 讓我閃耀 (1:37), 點心歌 (2:04), 跳飛機 (2:56). The long narrative ones (小時候, 象鼻尾貓, 詠鵝) are listening, not joining in.
