How to use it

A song only does the work if you stop singing. The whole method is:

  1. Sing it slowly, face to face, so he sees your mouth.
  2. Pause before the word you want and wait — count to five in your head.
  3. Accept anything: a gesture, a sound, the first letter, the whole word.
  4. Do the actions exaggeratedly — he can copy a hand before he can copy a word.
  5. Sing it again when he asks, even if it's the ninth time. That's the point.

The order runs warm-up → body → actions → animals/counting → story songs → feelings, so it works as a 20-minute session.

Why these

Every pick is named by at least one SLP source, and most by several:

  • One Little Finger — the single most-repeated favourite in the r/slp thread and in Speech Room News. Body parts plus a predictable "up / down".
  • Head Shoulders Knees And Toes — recommended by Wee Speech, Eyas Landing and Speech Room Resources for body-part vocabulary.
  • Five Little Ducks — an EI SLP in the thread: "All my clients LOVE 5 little ducks and filling in the 'quack quack quack' part." Best fill-in-the-gap song in the set.
  • Ten In The Bed / Five Little Monkeys — both from the thread's own shortlist; counting down plus "no more", an early negation.
  • Going on a Bear Hunt — the thread's pick for prepositions and following directions.
  • Slippery Fish — named twice in the thread as an EI favourite.
  • Baby Bumblebee — linked by an SLP in the thread, sung by Ms Rachel.

Notes

  • Overlap is deliberate. One Little Finger, Head Shoulders, Wheels, Itsy Bitsy and Five Little Ducks also sit on other cards. They're repeated because they're the ones the sources actually name — this card is a session, not a new collection.
  • Six tracks are new to the library: Five Little Monkeys, Ten In The Bed, Walking Walking, Bear Hunt, Slippery Fish, Baby Bumblebee.
  • Songs support, they don't replace assessment. If he's 2 and not joining in at all, that's a question for a speech therapist, not a playlist.
  • Keep screen time out of it: play the audio, not the video, and sing yourself. The research point in every source is interaction, not input.
