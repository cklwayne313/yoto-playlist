# Playlist ranking — best for him first

A single place to check before you put a card on the shelf. Ranked for **one
listener**: a 2-year-old boy with a possible speech delay, Cantonese at home,
English cards.

**Order = home language first, then speech value, then age fit** — with one
exception: a card he asks for by name gets shelf room, because a song played ten
times beats a better one played once. Fidelity only says how close the links are
to a real card; it does not change what he gets out of it. Nothing here is
arranged around a print run.

Last checked: **26 Nov 2026**.

## How to read this

| Column | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Speech** | Does it help talking? Repetition, short lines, actions, onomatopoeia, slow clear vocals. Not "is it nice music". |
| **Age 2** | Is the content right for a 2-year-old — pace, length, concepts. |
| **Fidelity** | How closely the YouTube links match the actual card. ★★★★★ = same recordings, durations within seconds. ★★ = right songs, different arrangements. `—` = no card exists. |

**Verdicts**

- **CORE** — daily rotation, the ones doing the work
- **GOOD** — solid, rotate in
- **FUN** — entertainment, not speech practice
- **LATER** — good card, wrong age for now

## What the order means

**Home language first.** Ranks 1–16 are all Cantonese, because the words he
needs next are Cantonese ones. English starts at 17.

Within a language the order is **speech value, then age fit**:

1. cards built around a named target — the three Cantonese therapy cards, then
   the seven English therapy cards;
2. slow, clear, one-voice music with exact recordings — Raffi, Caspar Babypants,
   Super Simple favourites;
3. theme albums where the nouns carry the work — vehicles, animals, food, farm,
   routines;
4. entertainment — Doggyland, Sesame, Blippi, the Wiggles;
5. seasonal cards, which are excellent for four weeks and then shelved;
6. film soundtracks and Kidz Bop, which are music rather than language input.

Two cards break the pattern on purpose. **silly-songs** is nonsense, but he asks
for it by name, and a favourite played ten times teaches more than a better card
played once. **cantonese-faith-songs** scores higher than its age fit because it
is Cantonese; it still waits, because a twelve-minute track is not a two-year-old
track.

This ranking is **for him, not for a print run.** The earlier version was bent to
keep exactly twenty cards inside a "shelf" set: that pushed four therapy cards
down to 21–24 and pulled the English music cards above them. That constraint is
gone, so the list is now the order you would actually introduce the cards in.

If you do print only part of the library, take them in rank order. 1–16 is the
Cantonese block and does most of the work; 17–23 is the English therapy set for
when he starts joining words; 24–28 is the music that is easy to leave playing.
Nothing below 36 is needed before he turns three.

## Re-checking later

Links rot and channels rename. To re-audit every link with real metadata:

```bash
YT=/home/wayne/Documents/youtube/.venv/bin/yt-dlp
grep -rhoE 'https://youtu\.be/[A-Za-z0-9_-]+' playlists/*/reference.md | sort -u \
  | sed 's#https://youtu.be/#https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=#' \
  | xargs $YT --skip-download --print '%(id)s | %(duration_string)s | %(uploader)s | %(title)s'
```

Anything that errors is dead. To catch a *wrong song* rather than a dead one,
compare the printed title and duration against the row in `reference.md`.
